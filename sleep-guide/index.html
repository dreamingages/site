The guide

From Morning to Moonrise

Small habits that may help, drawn from sleep-medicine guidance. Small numbers link to the sources.

  1. Morning
  2. 1
    Every morning

    Wake at the same time

    Pick one wake-up time and keep it, weekends included, even after a poor night. A steady anchor may help your body know when night is coming.124

  3. 2
    Soon after waking

    Step into morning light

    Time outdoors in the morning, or by a bright window, may help set your inner clock for an easier evening.105

  4. Afternoon
  5. 3
    Early afternoon

    Last coffee by early afternoon

    Caffeine can linger for hours. Many people sleep better with no tea, coffee or cola in the six or so hours before bed.942

  6. Evening
  7. 4
    With dinner

    Go easy on late meals and alcohol

    A big late meal can keep you uncomfortable, and a nightcap can make sleep lighter and more broken later in the night.2512

  8. 5
    1 hour before bed

    Dim the lights, set screens aside

    Soft lamps instead of bright overheads, and phones put down, can help your mind shift into night mode.2116

  9. 6
    Before you head up

    Write tomorrow’s to-do list

    Five minutes jotting down what’s on your mind, and what you’ll do about it tomorrow, may leave fewer thoughts to chase in bed.135

  10. Bedtime
  11. 7
    Your bedroom

    Cool, dark and quiet

    A slightly cool room (about 15–19°C or 60–67°F suits many people), blackout curtains or a mask, and earplugs or a steady soft sound may help you stay asleep.24614

  12. 8
    Lights out

    Slow your breathing

    Breathe in for 4, out for 6, for a few minutes. Or tense and release each muscle group in turn, from your toes upward.7156

  13. 9
    As you settle

    Give your mind a calm scene

    Picturing a peaceful place, or following a slow story at low volume, may crowd out the worries that keep you awake.166

  14. Night
  15. 10
    If you’re still awake

    Awake for a while? Get up and reset

    If you’ve been lying awake for what feels like 20 minutes, get up, sit somewhere dim and do something quiet, then return when sleepy. Skip the clock-watching.185

Try one change for a week or two before adding the next. Sleep tends to settle gradually, not overnight.

More help

More Help & Further Reading

Most sleep troubles ease with time and small changes. If yours don’t, help is available.

When to talk to someone

  • Poor sleep has gone on for a few months, or is making your days hard to cope with.4
  • Someone notices loud snoring, gasping, choking, or pauses in your breathing, or you feel very tired most days.17
  • You’ve tried changing your habits and nothing has shifted.4

There’s no need to wait until it’s “bad enough”. Asking early is sensible.

Start with your family doctor

A family doctor or GP can look for causes, such as medicines, pain or a sleep disorder, and refer you to a sleep clinic or a talking therapy if needed.4

  • US: AASM accredited sleep center directory
  • Canada: Canadian Sleep Society provider map
  • UK: start with your GP; see NHS: Insomnia

CBT-I programs

Cognitive behavioural therapy for insomnia (CBT-I) is the recommended first treatment for long-lasting insomnia.187

  • Insomnia Coach: a free, self-guided CBT-I app from the US VA
  • CBT-i Coach: free, designed to use alongside a clinician
  • Sleepio: often free through employers, health plans or the NHS where available
  • Sleepstation: UK; may be NHS-funded in some areas
