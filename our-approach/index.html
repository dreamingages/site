Dreaming Ages

Our Approach

How we make a calm night of history: restful enough to drift off to, careful enough to trust.

Why we tell it this way

Interesting, Never Exciting

A busy, worried mind is one of the main things that keeps people awake.1 In one sleep study, people with insomnia who pictured an engaging, peaceful scene fell asleep sooner than people given no instructions, while a vague “just distract yourself” didn’t help.2

So we aim for stories that are interesting enough to hold your attention, and calm enough to let go of: lamplit streets, markets at dusk, and the rhythms of ordinary life across the ages.

The sound

How an Episode Sounds

A slow, steady voice

The narration runs at about 115 words a minute, low and even, with room to breathe between sentences. Slower, lower, steadier voices are rated as more relaxing,34 and pauses help the body settle.5

No cliff-hangers

Suspense and emotional intensity raise arousal, especially in audio.67 So chapters don’t end on teasers, and the harder moments in history are told plainly, without graphic detail.

Nothing sudden

The voice stays at one steady level. Quiet music sits well beneath it and eases down as the episode goes on, with no stings and no loud outro, because sudden sounds tend to wake people up.8

No mid-video ad breaks

Nothing interrupts the story halfway through. An ad break is the last thing you want when you’ve just started to drift off.

The picture

Soft, Slow Images

Each episode is a slow series of painted-style scenes, with soft camera movement and no flashes or fast cuts. The pictures change more often in the first few minutes, then settle to about one a minute as the night goes on.

Bright screens can delay sleep,9 so feel free to dim the screen, turn it off, or listen as a podcast. The story doesn’t need your eyes.

Length and shape

A Whole Evening, Gently Paced

Our episodes are long enough to fall asleep to, usually about an hour or more. Each one opens with a short, calm welcome and a single soft reminder to subscribe, follows a few clear chapters (with timestamps in the description), and closes with a short thank-you and a quiet goodnight.

Research and accuracy

Restful, but True

  1. It starts with reading. Each episode is researched from named histories, translations of ancient and medieval sources, and archaeological studies. The full list is in every episode’s description.
  2. Key facts are checked. Each episode is researched from published sources, and we check key facts before it goes public.
  3. We say where historians disagree. If the evidence is thin or debated, the narration says so. Imagined scenes are signposted (“Picture…”, “Imagine…”), so they’re never mistaken for fact.
  4. Names are said properly. Unfamiliar names are checked against dictionaries and native-speaker recordings before the narration is made.
Honest about AI

How We Use AI

We’d rather tell you plainly.

  • The narrator is an AI voice. It’s made with Kokoro, an open-source text-to-speech model, and softened for sleep. We know it isn’t perfect yet.
  • The images are AI-generated. They’re artistic impressions, not historical records or photographs.
  • AI tools help us research and draft. A person chooses each topic, approves every script, and listens to every episode in full before it goes public.
  • It’s labelled. Every episode is marked on YouTube as containing altered or synthetic content. Music and soft ambient sounds are credited in each description.

AI lets a small channel make long, carefully sourced episodes three nights a week. It doesn’t replace checking the history, which is why we always show our sources.

What the research doesn’t say

An Honest Note

No study has tested history episodes as a sleep aid. In the largest trial of bedtime stories, with 495 adults, stories helped about as much as other calm audio, not more.10 Calm audio and music mostly improve how rested people feel, rather than sleep measured in a lab.11 Still, many people find stories a lovely way to wind down. In a survey of over a thousand bedtime audiobook listeners, most said it helps them fall asleep.12

We think that’s reason enough, and we’ll never claim our episodes treat sleep problems. If poor sleep lasts for weeks or affects your days, please talk to a doctor.
